Travel Guides & Destination Insights: How to Research, Plan, and Arrive Fully Prepared

📖 12 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

A destination guide is only valuable if it tells you what travel actually looks like — not the polished brochure version, not the filtered highlight reel, but the real texture of a place: what the airport is like at 2am, which neighbourhood is genuinely safe versus which just looks good on a map, and what locals think about the tourist circuit that dominates every "top 10" list.

At REO Travels, we've helped thousands of Nigerians travel internationally over the years. This guide distils what we've learned about how to research a destination properly — before you book anything.

Start With the Entry Requirements, Not the Attractions

This sounds obvious, but the single most common source of trip disruption is a document or requirement the traveller discovered too late. Before you research hotels or flights, establish the basics: Does your destination require a visa? How long does it take to obtain? Are there vaccination requirements? Is your passport valid for six months beyond your return date? What is the current travel advisory for your destination?

The Nigerian passport grants visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to a growing but still limited number of countries. For most popular destinations — Europe, North America, East Asia — you will need a visa, and the application process takes time. Build that into your planning timeline before anything else.

Choosing a Destination That Matches Your Travel Style

Every destination has a personality, and it either matches yours or it doesn't. Dubai rewards travellers who enjoy organised luxury, urban spectacle, and curated experiences. Morocco rewards those who want sensory immersion, history, and negotiation. Zanzibar rewards those who can slow down. The Maldives rewards those who want very little beyond beauty and water. Knowing which kind of traveller you are prevents the expensive mistake of booking the wrong trip.

  • If you want culture and history: Egypt, Morocco, Turkey, Ethiopia, India, Portugal
  • If you want natural beauty: Zanzibar, Maldives, Rwanda, Iceland, Thailand
  • If you want urban energy: Dubai, London, New York, Istanbul, Singapore
  • If you want beach and relaxation: Maldives, Seychelles, Zanzibar, Bali, Mauritius
  • If you want short-haul African travel: Accra, Kigali, Nairobi, Abidjan, Cape Town

Understanding Seasonality

Most destinations have a best time to visit, a shoulder season, and a period to avoid. Travelling in peak season guarantees the best weather but the highest prices and the largest crowds. Shoulder seasons — the weeks immediately before and after peak season — often offer 80% of the experience for 50–60% of the cost. Understanding this pattern for your specific destination can save significant money without materially affecting your experience.

For East African destinations like Kenya and Tanzania, the dry seasons (January–March and June–October) align with the best wildlife viewing. For European cities, spring (April–May) and early autumn (September–October) offer the best combination of weather, crowd levels, and prices. For Dubai, November through February is categorically the right time to visit.

What a Good Destination Guide Should Tell You

Beyond the attractions checklist, a useful destination guide answers: What is the local currency and can you access it easily? Is tap water safe to drink? What is the tipping culture? What are the local laws that might surprise a visitor (public alcohol consumption, dress codes, photography restrictions)? What does a realistic daily budget look like — not the minimum backpacker budget and not the five-star budget, but a real, comfortable mid-range day?

It should also tell you what doesn't work. The restaurant the influencer visited is often not the best restaurant. The "hidden gem" that went viral is no longer hidden. The hotel that got glowing reviews in 2021 may have changed management. Always cross-reference guide content with recent traveller reviews, ideally from travellers with a similar profile to your own.

How REO Travels Builds Your Destination Brief

For every client booking through REO Travels, we provide a destination brief specific to their itinerary — covering entry requirements, local norms, weather at time of travel, neighbourhood recommendations, and practical logistics. This isn't a generic PDF; it's built around your specific travel dates, accommodation, and interests. Many clients tell us it's one of the most useful things they receive from us.

The Most Overlooked Part of Destination Research: What to Do on the Ground

The best travel experiences are almost never the ones that are most heavily promoted. They're the food market a local mentioned at breakfast, the small museum you walked past and decided to enter on a whim, the neighbourhood you explored because your hotel was ten minutes from the main tourist circuit. Leave room in every itinerary for the unplanned. The most memorable travel stories rarely begin with "we booked this three months in advance."

Travel Tips & Smart Packing: What Experienced Travellers Always Carry (and What They Always Leave Behind)

📖 8 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

Packing is one of those skills that sounds simple until you're standing at a check-in desk being told your bag is 4kg over the limit, or rummaging through everything at 11pm looking for the one adapter you forgot. After years of watching clients navigate airports and hotels, we've noticed that the most seasoned travellers share a remarkably consistent philosophy: carry less than you think you need, and carry it smarter.

This guide reflects what actually works — not the aspirational 10-item capsule wardrobe, but practical principles that apply to real trips, especially from Nigeria to international destinations.

The Most Important Packing Principle: Luggage Rules First

Before you pull out a single item of clothing, know your airline's baggage allowance — both for checked luggage and carry-on. This is especially critical on multi-leg journeys where different airlines may apply different rules. Many international flights out of Lagos operate under generous allowances, but the connecting flight on a regional carrier may impose a 7kg carry-on restriction that catches travellers off guard. Check every airline on your itinerary, not just the main one.

As a general rule: if you can manage your trip in carry-on only, do. You eliminate baggage fees, you eliminate the risk of lost luggage, and you move through airports in a fraction of the time. For trips under two weeks to destinations with easy access to laundry or dry cleaning, carry-on is almost always sufficient.

What Every Traveller Should Carry (Always)

  • Universal travel adapter: A good multi-region adapter with at least two USB ports. Not all hotel rooms have accessible sockets, and European, UK, and American plugs are all different from Nigerian ones.
  • Portable power bank (10,000mAh minimum): Airports drain phones. Long-haul flights drain phones. A reliable power bank means you are never navigating a new city with 3% battery.
  • Compression packing cubes: These are not optional for organised travellers. They separate clothing categories, compress volume significantly, and make unpacking and repacking exponentially easier. Buy them once; use them forever.
  • Ziplock bags (various sizes): For toiletries, wet items, snacks, and the inevitable airport security scan. Underrated, underused.
  • A slim cross-body or anti-theft bag: For documents, cash, and phone in busy city environments. Keeps your passport accessible without putting it in a back pocket or checked luggage.
  • Printed copies of all key documents: Flight confirmations, hotel bookings, visa documents, travel insurance. Apps fail, batteries die, airports have bad Wi-Fi. Paper doesn't.
  • Reusable water bottle: Fill it after airport security. Saves money constantly, especially in destinations where bottled water is expensive.
  • Basic medication kit: Paracetamol, antihistamine, antidiarrheal, plasters, and any personal prescription medication in its original labelled packaging. Airport pharmacies are expensive; not every destination has one.

The Clothing Formula That Actually Works

For a 7–14 day trip, experienced travellers typically pack using the following as a baseline: seven days of underwear and socks (lightweight, quick-dry), three or four versatile bottom pieces (mix of casual and smart casual), five or six tops that work across contexts, one lightweight jacket or layer suitable for both air conditioning and mild outdoor temperature, and one outfit that can serve a formal or semi-formal occasion without being exclusively formal. Everything should be able to mix with everything else.

What most people over-pack: shoes. Two pairs solve most travel situations — one comfortable walking shoe and one that can be dressed up. The second pair goes into a shoe bag to prevent it soiling clothing.

Smart Packing for Nigerian Travellers Specifically

Nigerian passport holders are statistically more likely to face secondary screening at certain international airports. This is a reality of the current travel environment. Having your documents — visa, return ticket, hotel confirmation, proof of funds — immediately accessible in a clearly organised folder (not buried in your checked luggage) reduces screening time and communicates organisation and preparedness to the officer. First impressions at immigration matter.

If you are travelling to a cold destination — Europe in winter, Canada, the UK — take the cold seriously. Layering is more effective than single heavy items: thermal base layer, mid-layer fleece, outer windproof and waterproof jacket. Nigerian body temperatures are calibrated to heat; genuine cold in an unfamiliar environment is uncomfortable in a way that can affect your entire trip.

What to Leave Behind

The items that consistently stay behind after one bad experience: hair dryers (hotels almost always have them), full-size toiletry bottles (decant into 100ml travel bottles or buy on arrival), "just in case" outfits that get worn zero times, multiple pairs of jeans (they are heavy, they take forever to dry, and two pairs serve as effectively as four), and physical books (a Kindle or reading app on your phone weighs nothing).

The Final Check Before You Zip Up

Lay everything out. Now remove two items you packed "just in case." Now check: passport, visa, flight tickets, accommodation confirmation, travel insurance certificate, yellow fever card (for Nigerian travellers — non-negotiable), adapter, power bank, and any prescription medication. Those items are your non-negotiables. Everything else is negotiable.

Hidden Gems & Must-Visit Places: Africa's Most Underrated Destinations You Should Already Have on Your List

📖 10 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

The continent that contains Nigeria is also home to some of the world's most extraordinary travel destinations — and most of them are not on the average Nigerian traveller's radar. Cape Town, Nairobi, and Cairo dominate the conversation, and they are all genuinely worth visiting. But there is a second tier of African destinations that consistently produces the most enthusiastic feedback from our clients: places that are beautiful, accessible, culturally rich, and refreshingly free of the overcrowding that has begun to affect the continent's most famous cities.

These are the destinations our team recommends when a client says they want to see Africa differently.

Kigali, Rwanda — Africa's Most Surprising Capital City

Rwanda's capital is frequently described by first-time visitors as the cleanest, most orderly city they have ever seen in Africa. This is not accidental — it reflects a national commitment to transformation that is visible in everything from the immaculate streets to the quality of the service industry to the remarkable safety record that allows tourists to walk freely at night. Kigali is a city that rewards the curious traveller: the Genocide Memorial is one of the most important and moving historical sites on the continent; the Kimironko Market is the kind of authentic, non-touristy market experience most African cities no longer offer; and the restaurant and hotel scene has matured enormously in recent years.

Beyond Kigali, Rwanda's gorilla trekking in Volcanoes National Park is one of Africa's most extraordinary wildlife experiences — and one of the most exclusive, with permits priced to limit numbers and protect the gorilla population. For Nigerian passport holders, Rwanda offers visa on arrival, making it one of the most accessible international destinations on the continent.

Diani Beach, Kenya — The Indian Ocean as it Should Be

While most Kenyan travellers head straight for the Maasai Mara, Diani — a 35km stretch of white sand coast about an hour south of Mombasa — has quietly become one of East Africa's finest beach destinations. The water is warm, impossibly blue, and sheltered by a reef that keeps conditions gentle for swimming. The beach is wide enough to never feel crowded. The accommodation options range from backpacker bandas to genuinely luxurious private villas at a fraction of what equivalent properties cost in the Maldives or Seychelles. And the nearby Shimba Hills National Reserve offers the rare combination of coastal forest hiking and wildlife viewing within 30 minutes of the beach.

Dakar, Senegal — West Africa's Most Cosmopolitan City

Dakar sits at the westernmost point of the African continent, jutting into the Atlantic with an energy that is unlike any other West African capital. It is simultaneously historically significant — Gorée Island, a few kilometres offshore, was one of the primary departure points of the Atlantic slave trade and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and vibrantly contemporary, with a thriving music scene, a growing contemporary art culture, and some of the best seafood on the continent.

For Nigerians, Dakar is a fascinating cultural parallel — a major West African city with different colonial and culinary influences, French as the official language, a dominant Muslim identity, and a relaxed, creative atmosphere that many visitors find unexpectedly refreshing. The Pink Lake (Lac Rose), the IFAN Museum of African Arts, and the bustling Sandaga market are among the highlights.

The Simien Mountains, Ethiopia — Africa's Roof

Ethiopia is one of Africa's most historically rich countries — the only African nation never colonised, home to one of the world's oldest Christian traditions, and the origin of the coffee plant. The Simien Mountains in the north represent some of the most dramatic highland scenery on the continent: jagged escarpments dropping thousands of metres, Gelada baboons in large troops on the clifftops, and the endemic Ethiopian wolf in the high-altitude moorlands. Trekking in the Simiens is genuinely world-class, and the infrastructure has improved significantly over the past decade.

Pemba Island, Tanzania — Zanzibar Without the Crowds

Zanzibar's more famous neighbour gets most of the attention — but Pemba Island, 80km to the north, offers many of the same elements (turquoise water, Swahili culture, coral reefs) with a fraction of the tourist footprint. Pemba is known among divers as one of the finest sites in the Indian Ocean, with pristine reef walls, strong currents that bring large pelagic species, and water visibility that regularly exceeds 30 metres. Above water, it is lush and hilly where Zanzibar is flat, with clove and coconut plantations covering the interior. Accommodation options are limited but exceptional for those who seek them out.

A Note on Getting There

Most of these destinations are served from Lagos via Nairobi, Addis Ababa, or direct regional routes. Journey times range from 3–8 hours total. REO Travels handles connections, visa requirements, and accommodation for all of these destinations — and for several of them, our regional partnerships allow us to offer pricing that is genuinely competitive with what you'd find booking independently.

Travel Stories & Experiences: What Our Clients Taught Us About the Journeys That Actually Change You

📖 7 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

After years of organising travel for thousands of Nigerians — first-timers boarding an international flight for the first time, and seasoned globetrotters who've been to forty countries — we've started noticing patterns in which trips people actually remember. Not just enjoyed at the time, but genuinely carry with them.

It turns out the formula for a transformative travel experience is more consistent than you'd expect — and it has very little to do with budget.

The First International Trip Story (Almost) Everyone Tells

We've heard hundreds of versions of the same story: a client books their first international trip — often Dubai or London — and arrives expecting something dramatic. The country they land in is different, but the first few hours are surprisingly familiar: an airport, a taxi, a hotel. And then something happens. It might be a moment of getting genuinely lost with no data connection. Or eating something they can't identify in a market they stumbled into. Or striking up a conversation with someone whose entire frame of reference is different from theirs. And they return to Nigeria changed in a way that's difficult to articulate but impossible to miss.

First international trips are almost always disproportionately significant. They establish that the world is larger and more accessible than it appeared from inside Nigeria's borders — and that realisation tends to compound. Most clients who book their second trip with us do so within six months of their first.

The Honeymoon That Becomes the Marriage's Foundation

We organise a significant number of honeymoon trips each year — to the Maldives, Zanzibar, Mauritius, Bali, and elsewhere. What strikes us consistently is that couples return speaking less about the destination's beauty and more about what the shared experience gave their relationship. Navigating a new country together, making decisions about what to do each day, sitting together somewhere genuinely beautiful with no work notifications and no extended family: couples consistently describe honeymoon travel as the period in which they understood what their marriage was going to be.

The destination matters less than the design. A well-planned trip that includes the right mix of exploration and genuine rest, private moments and shared experiences, is the goal — not the most expensive resort or the most Instagrammable location.

The Solo Trip That Wasn't Supposed to Be Significant

A client — a business owner from Port Harcourt — booked a week in Istanbul through us last year. Primarily a work recalibration trip, she told us when she booked. She wanted to be alone somewhere beautiful and think. She came back and immediately started planning her next three trips. What happened in Istanbul? She walked across the Galata Bridge, ate at a meyhane in Beyoğlu with strangers who insisted she join their table, visited the Süleymaniye Mosque at sunset, and spent an afternoon in a hammam that had been operating for 400 years. Nothing dramatic. Everything meaningful.

Solo travel has a particular quality that group and couples travel cannot replicate — a vulnerability and openness to unplanned connection that changes how you move through a city. We're increasingly building solo itineraries for clients who want this experience with the logistical safety net of professional support underneath them.

The Family Trip That Became a Tradition

Some of our most consistent returning clients are families who took their first international trip together five or eight years ago and have made annual family travel a fixture ever since. What started as a one-off adventure to Accra or Abidjan evolved into a tradition that now defines how their children understand the world. Children who travel internationally young tend to develop a particular kind of curiosity and confidence — an intuition that differences between people and places are interesting rather than threatening.

What the Best Travel Experiences Have in Common

Looking across every story our clients have shared with us, the trips that genuinely stay with people tend to share a few characteristics: they include at least one moment of genuine disorientation — something that pushed the traveller outside their familiar operating mode; they involve real connection with the place rather than a curated surface experience; they include enough unscheduled time for the unexpected to happen; and they are approached with openness rather than a fixed agenda of photographs to take and boxes to tick.

The most memorable travel experiences are rarely the ones that went exactly to plan. They're the ones where something went slightly sideways and you discovered, somewhat to your own surprise, that you could handle it.

Your Story Is Waiting

Every person who has ever had a transformative travel experience was once a person who hadn't yet left. The trip that changes something for you exists — it just hasn't happened yet. Our job is to remove every logistical obstacle between you and that experience.

What kind of travel story do you want to tell? Let our team help you design a trip that delivers it.

Visa, Flights & Travel Updates: The Key Changes Nigerian Travellers Need to Know in 2025

📖 6 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

For Nigerian passport holders, 2025 has brought a mix of developments — some welcome, some requiring immediate awareness. Visa policies shift without warning. New airline routes open while others are suspended. Entry requirements that were relaxed during the post-pandemic recovery period have in some cases been reinstated. Staying current is not optional; it is the difference between a smooth journey and a denied boarding pass.

Here is what our team is tracking as the most significant updates for Nigerian travellers right now.

Schengen Visa: Appointment Backlogs and the New Biometric Requirements

Appointment availability at European embassies in Lagos and Abuja remains a significant bottleneck. Several embassies are operating with backlogs of four to eight weeks for first-available appointments, meaning travellers who begin their application process less than ten weeks before their intended departure date risk missing it entirely even if their application is subsequently approved without issue. If you are planning European travel in 2025, begin the visa process no later than twelve weeks before your travel date.

The EU has also been rolling out its new Entry/Exit System (EES), which replaces manual passport stamping with biometric digital registration at Schengen borders. Implementation has been phased and delayed in some countries, but travellers should be aware that additional processing time at points of entry may result as the system becomes active across more Schengen member states.

UK Visitor Visa: Important Changes to Financial Evidence Requirements

The UK Home Office has not made headline-level changes to Nigerian visitor visa policy in 2025, but refusal rates remain high and the standard of financial evidence expected has increased in practice. Applications supported by bank statements showing a consistent salary credit pattern from a verifiable employer are performing significantly better than applications relying on large lump-sum balances or business accounts without corroborating documentation. If you are applying for a UK visitor visa, the supporting letter and financial evidence presentation matter as much as the underlying numbers.

UAE (Dubai): Visa on Arrival No Longer Available for Nigerian Passports

This is perhaps the most important change for Nigerian travellers to be aware of. Nigerian passport holders previously had access to a visa on arrival facility for the UAE under certain conditions. This arrangement is no longer in effect. All Nigerian passport holders must obtain a UAE tourist visa in advance of travel. Processing through an authorised agent takes three to five working days under normal conditions. Do not book non-refundable flights to Dubai without a confirmed visa in hand.

Saudi Arabia Umrah Visa: Expanded Group Travel for Women

Saudi Arabia has continued its policy liberalisation around Umrah travel for women. As of 2024–2025, women of all ages may perform Umrah in organised groups without a mahram (male guardian), provided they travel with a licensed group operator. This has meaningfully expanded access for Nigerian Muslim women who previously faced logistical barriers to performing Umrah independently. REO Travels is authorised to process Umrah visas and operates organised Umrah groups from Lagos throughout the year.

Kenya: eVisa System Now Mandatory

Kenya has fully transitioned to a mandatory eVisa system for all travellers, including Nigerian passport holders. Visas on arrival at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport and other Kenyan ports of entry are no longer issued. Applications must be submitted online through the official eCitizen portal before travel. Processing typically takes two to five working days, though peak periods may extend this. The fee is currently USD 50 for a single-entry tourist visa.

New Airline Routes and Capacity Updates

Several airlines have adjusted their Nigeria operations in the past year. Ethiopian Airlines has increased frequency on the Lagos–Addis route, improving onward connection options to East African and Asian destinations. Air Peace has continued expanding its international network and now operates direct routes to a growing number of regional and continental destinations. On the other hand, some legacy carriers have reduced capacity on the Lagos–London route, contributing to higher fares than travellers saw in 2022–2023. Booking international flights twelve to sixteen weeks in advance consistently yields the best pricing in the current environment.

Yellow Fever Certificate: Don't Forget the Physical Card

This is a perennial issue but worth repeating in 2025. Nigeria is an endemic yellow fever country, and dozens of destination countries require proof of vaccination for Nigerian passport holders. The requirement is specifically for the physical International Certificate of Vaccination — the yellow booklet. Digital records, NHS app entries, or verbal attestation are not accepted at immigration. If you cannot produce a valid, stamped yellow fever card at arrival, you may be denied entry or quarantined. If your card is lost, expired, or was never obtained, address this before you travel — not at the departure gate.

Stay Updated Through REO Travels

Our team monitors changes to visa requirements, airline operations, and entry conditions continuously. Clients who book through REO Travels receive updated briefings relevant to their specific destination and travel dates — not generic information that may be months out of date. Travel requirements change faster than any published guide can track; the safest approach is to verify with your travel consultant within 72 hours of departure.

Don't let an avoidable policy change disrupt your trip. Our team stays current so you don't have to — and we'll flag anything that affects your booking.

Budget Travel & Luxury Escapes: How to Get the Most From Any Travel Budget — Whatever It Is

📖 5 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

The biggest misconception in travel planning is that more money automatically means a better experience. In reality, value in travel is almost entirely a function of planning quality — knowing where to spend, where to save, how to time a booking, and which splurges actually change the experience versus which ones simply cost more for equivalent results.

We work with clients across a wide range of budgets, from first-time travellers managing every naira carefully to high-net-worth individuals for whom cost is secondary to quality and exclusivity. Here is what we've learned about how to maximise travel value at every point on that spectrum.

Budget Travel: Where Smart Planning Beats Higher Spending

The most impactful budget decision in international travel is usually the flight — and flight pricing is genuinely dynamic. For most popular long-haul routes from Lagos, fares twelve to sixteen weeks out are consistently lower than fares purchased within six weeks of departure. The savings are not marginal: on Lagos–London or Lagos–Dubai routes, early booking can mean a difference of ₦150,000–300,000 per person on the same airline and routing. If your travel dates have any flexibility, Tuesday and Wednesday departures typically price lower than Friday and Sunday.

Budget accommodation is more contextually dependent. In cities like Dubai and Singapore, the hotel tier is visible in the experience — premium-location mid-range hotels genuinely provide a different city experience than out-of-centre budget options. In destinations like Zanzibar or the Maldives, however, a well-reviewed guesthouse on a local island can provide a more authentic and enjoyable experience than a mid-tier resort at three times the price. Know which context you're in before defaulting to the cheapest option.

The Budget Moves That Actually Matter

  • Book flights early, not accommodation: Hotel pricing is generally more negotiable and more dynamic than flight pricing. Lock in your flights; negotiate your rooms.
  • Travel in shoulder season: The six weeks either side of peak season typically offer 30–50% savings on accommodation without materially affecting the experience. This is the single highest-return budget decision available for most destinations.
  • Eat where locals eat: In almost every international destination, the best food is not in tourist-facing restaurants. It's in the street market, the neighbourhood canteen, the lunch spot near the local office district. This is also where you'll have the most interesting experiences.
  • Use airport transfers strategically: Pre-booking an airport transfer is almost always cheaper than taking a metered taxi or ride-hailing app on arrival, especially in cities where surge pricing or foreign-looking visitors attract premium rates.
  • Consider package bookings: For destinations like the Maldives, Zanzibar, or Umrah, a properly negotiated package (flights + accommodation + transfers) frequently costs less than booking each component individually, because operators bundle discounts across the chain. REO Travels can demonstrate the comparison on any destination.

Luxury Travel: What Premium Actually Means

In luxury travel, the question is never "how much does it cost?" — it's "does it deliver an experience that justifies the cost?" The most expensive hotel in a city is not automatically the best hotel. The correct question is: what does this property do exceptionally well, and does that align with what I actually want from this trip?

For high-end accommodation, the attributes that consistently justify premium pricing are: location (a hotel that puts you within walking distance of what matters genuinely changes the experience), service culture (properties where staff remember your name and anticipate rather than react), and unique physical attributes that cannot be replicated at lower price points — an overwater villa, a rooftop infinity pool with a particular view, a historic building with genuine character.

Luxury Experiences Worth Every Naira

  • Business class flights on long-haul routes (8+ hours): The gap between economy and business on a 10-hour flight is the gap between arriving exhausted and arriving functional. For business trips or trips where you need to perform on arrival, this is not an indulgence — it is an investment.
  • Private villa accommodation in the Maldives or Seychelles: These destinations are built around the private, exclusive experience. Half-measures here rarely satisfy; if the budget allows, a genuine overwater or beachfront villa changes the experience categorically.
  • Private guided tours of major sites: In Cairo, Rome, Istanbul, or Marrakech, a knowledgeable private guide transforms a sightseeing checklist into a genuine historical and cultural experience. The cost is typically $50–150 for a half day — modest in the context of a serious trip.
  • A once-in-a-kind experience budget: Gorilla trekking in Rwanda, a seaplane transfer in the Maldives, a private dhow cruise in Zanzibar. These are the moments that define the trip in memory. Building a dedicated budget for one exceptional experience is almost always worth it.

How REO Travels Approaches Value Across Budget Levels

Our job is to build the best possible trip within the budget a client brings us — not to upsell, and not to cut corners that affect the experience. When a client tells us their budget, we work back from what that budget can realistically achieve at their destination, and we are honest when expectations and budget don't align so we can find a solution together. We've built extraordinary trips on very modest budgets, and we've built exceptional luxury experiences for clients who wanted the best. The quality of the planning is the same in both cases.

Tell us your destination, your dates, and your budget. We'll show you exactly what's possible — and how to get the most from every naira you spend.

Performing Umrah from Nigeria: A Step-by-Step Pilgrim's Guide

📖 9 min read✍️ REO Travels Team

Umrah is a voluntary act of worship that can be performed at any time of year. Unlike Hajj — which is obligatory for Muslims who are physically and financially able, and takes place on fixed dates in Dhul Hijjah — Umrah carries immense spiritual merit and is accessible to anyone with the means and desire to undertake it. For Nigerian Muslims, it represents one of life's most profound journeys. This guide walks through every practical consideration.

The Umrah Visa

Saudi Arabia requires a specific Umrah visa for pilgrims — tourist visas cannot be used to perform Umrah. The visa must be processed through an accredited Umrah operator (known as a licensed travel agent). In Nigeria, these agents are registered with the National Hajj Commission of Nigeria (NAHCON). REO Travels is authorised to process Umrah visas on behalf of pilgrims, handling the full submission to the Saudi consulate.

Processing typically takes 7–14 working days. Peak periods — particularly the weeks before Ramadan — can extend this significantly. We strongly recommend beginning your visa process at least 6 weeks before your intended travel date.

Required Documents

  • Valid international passport with minimum 6 months' validity from the date of return
  • Completed Umrah visa application form
  • Two recent passport photographs (white background, biometric standard, no head covering for men — women should show the face clearly with hijab if worn)
  • Meningitis ACWY vaccination certificate — this is mandatory. Saudi Arabia requires proof of meningococcal vaccination taken no more than 3 years before travel. The vaccination is available at designated government hospitals.
  • Polio vaccination certificate for Nigerian travellers — Nigeria remains on the Saudi polio vaccination requirement list. Proof of polio vaccination (typically in a yellow booklet) must be presented.
  • For women under 45 travelling without a mahram (husband or close male relative): a letter of consent from the mahram and documentation of the group they are travelling with. Saudi Umrah regulations have been significantly relaxed in recent years, and women over 45 can now travel in organised groups without a mahram.
  • Marriage certificate if travelling as a couple (may be requested)

When to Perform Umrah

  • Ramadan: The most spiritually rewarding time. The Prophet (ﷺ) described Umrah during Ramadan as equivalent in reward to Hajj performed with him. Masjid Al-Haram is at its most electric — nightly Tarawih prayers, the sense of collective devotion, and Laylatul Qadr in the final ten nights. It is also the most expensive and most crowded period. Book 4–6 months in advance.
  • Rajab and Sha'ban (the months before Ramadan): The best balance of spiritual atmosphere and manageable crowds. Prices begin to rise in Sha'ban as Ramadan approaches.
  • Off-peak (Muharram, Safar, Rabi'): Significantly lower prices, much thinner crowds, and easier access to the Kaaba for tawaf. The spiritual experience is no less valid — and some scholars prefer quieter periods for the focus they allow.

What to Expect on Arrival

Most Nigerian pilgrims fly into Jeddah's King Abdulaziz International Airport (JED), approximately 1 hour's drive from Makkah. Some packages fly into Madinah (MED) and begin in the city of the Prophet (ﷺ) before proceeding to Makkah for Umrah — this sequence allows pilgrims to perform Umrah in a state of spiritual preparation and is considered spiritually beneficial.

Upon arrival, your tour operator's representative will meet you, handle immigration and baggage, and transport you directly to your hotel. Hotel proximity to Masjid Al-Haram varies by package — 5-star packages typically place you within 50–200 metres of the Haram, while standard packages may be a 15–20 minute walk. The Makkah Metro (Al-Mashaaer Al-Muqaddassah) and shuttle buses serve pilgrims staying further out.
